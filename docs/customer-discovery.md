# Customer discovery: MSP handovers

## Hypothesis

When a client changes MSPs, the outgoing provider, incoming provider, and client often coordinate through separate PSA tickets, email, spreadsheets, and document exports. The gap may be a neutral record of requests, evidence, open exceptions, support boundaries, and the date responsibility changes.

This is a hypothesis, not a validated market or revenue claim. Existing PSAs and documentation products already support onboarding projects and checklists.

## Who to interview

Start with MSP owners, service-delivery leads, or technicians who personally handled at least one client transition in the last year. Interview outgoing and incoming providers separately where possible. Do not ask for client names, credentials, contracts, or confidential documents.

## Ask about the last real transition

1. Tell me about the most recent client transition you worked on. What triggered it, and what happened first?
2. Which parties were involved, and who coordinated the work?
3. What tools and artifacts did you use? Which parts were tracked in your PSA, email, spreadsheet, or documents?
4. What information or evidence was missing, late, duplicated, or disputed?
5. What happened after the handover packet was delivered? How many follow-up questions or work hours did it create?
6. At what exact point did responsibility change? Who agreed to that boundary, and where was the agreement recorded?
7. Was there a support overlap? Who paid for it, and what work fell outside the agreed scope?
8. What went wrong or created risk? What did it cost in time, money, service impact, or client trust?
9. How many transitions do you handle in a typical year, and how does that vary?
10. What did you try to improve this process? Why did that work or fail?

Ask for concrete examples and sequence. Avoid pitching the prototype until the interviewee has described their current workflow and problems.

## Test a solution only after the workflow discussion

Show the prototype's owner, due date, evidence reference, exception, cutover boundary, and export fields. Ask:

- Which part, if any, would change your next transition?
- Which existing system would this need to fit alongside?
- How do you buy tools for this workflow today? Who owns the budget and approves a purchase?
- What staff time or tool spend does this process currently require, and what would a new tool need to replace or improve?
- Which party would initiate the purchase: the client, outgoing MSP, incoming MSP, or someone else?
- What security, retention, or contractual requirements would prevent using it?
- What would need to be true for you to use it in an upcoming transition?

Record actual commitments separately from interest. Stronger evidence includes permission to follow up on a named upcoming workflow, a concrete introduction to the budget owner, or willingness to run a paid pilot. Do not treat compliments, hypothetical pricing, or survey votes as purchases.

## Decision after interviews

Continue discovery only if multiple independent MSPs describe the same cross-company coordination or cutover problem, identify a budget owner, and agree to a concrete next step. If their existing PSA templates solve the issue, stop building a standalone checklist. If they want a shared workspace, validate tenant separation, access control, audit history, retention, and secure evidence handling before adding hosted collaboration.
